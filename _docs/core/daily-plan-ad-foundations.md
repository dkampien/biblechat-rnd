# Daily Plan Ad Foundations

The product knowledge that ads are built from. BibleChat's marketing focuses on one feature, the daily plan, so this doc covers only that. It exists because a diagnosed failure of past ad systems was product shallowness: ads bridged a user's pain to the feature with vague promises because nobody had written down what the feature concretely does.

## 1. Positioning

BibleChat is faith-shaped mental health. The buyer is not looking for a Bible app; they are a Christian with a life problem (marriage strain, anxiety, grief, guilt, loneliness) for whom faith is the natural way through. Ads therefore never start from the product. They start from the person's problem, in their own words, and dress the daily plan as the way through it.

The product is intangible: its benefits cannot be conveyed by showing the interface as a feature tour. What can be shown is the problem, the mechanism, and the outcome.

## 2. The three assets of intangible selling

Every ad is some arrangement of these three, and each has a source:

- **The pain moment.** A specific scene, not a category: lying awake at 2am replaying the fight, crying in the car outside church. Source: the VoC corpus (53 problems, 343 angles), used verbatim or near-verbatim. The hook is usually the user's own sentence said back to them.
- **The mechanism.** What concretely happens when they use the daily plan (see the mechanism card below). This is the credibility bridge between their problem and the feature; without it the ad collapses into a vague promise, and vague promises die.
- **The proof.** A carrier the viewer believes: testimony, transformation story, "I was skeptical" framing. Intangible outcomes need a human voucher.

**The demonstrable exception: UI as scene.** A feature tour is dead, but the interface can carry an ad when it is the set of a story rather than the subject: a screen recording of someone typing their real pain into the chat at 2am and receiving a caring, scriptural reply. The daily plan equivalent is the ritual shot: day 1, day 2, day 3 of a plan on screen while the story arcs over it.

## 3. Mechanism card: the daily plan

The card is done when anyone writing an ad for any of the 53 pains can complete this sentence without inventing anything: "You get ___ every day, which does ___ for your [pain] in ___ minutes."

Content (all TODO; every claim concrete enough to show on screen, written in customer language):

1. The ritual, one sentence: what a plan day consists of and how many minutes.
2. One worked example: a real plan for one pain, day 1 / day 3 / day 7, three bullets each. The heart of the card; ads for other pains transpose its shape.
3. Personalization, one line: what actually adapts to the user's problem.
4. Trial and price. Known (via premium screen + July data, unverified): weekly $2.2/week with trial; monthly $4.4/month with trial (suspected decoy tier); yearly without trial at "$1 first three months, then $33" (effective ~$36/year). Roughly 40% trial-to-paid (July: 80k trials, 37k subs). Still TODO: trial length; where the paywall hits (evidence points to in-app after install, no web paywall, but unconfirmed).
5. Outcome lines: 3-5 real user quotes on what it did for them (source: VoC purchase triggers, store reviews).

Sourcing: items 1-3 need one real pass through the app or its plan content (repo or onboarding tooling). Items 4-5 are public or in the VoC tools.

## 4. Scope decisions on record

- Daily plan only. All marketing focuses on this feature; other features are documented only if that changes.
- No separate ICP document. The 53 payer-proven problems are the customer profile in behavioral form; personas, if ever needed, fall out of clustering them.
- The pain-moment library is the VoC corpus itself, referenced, not duplicated here.
- Targeting starts at age 35+.
