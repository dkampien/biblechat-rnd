# How the numbers work (app-only version, superseded by ../02-how-the-numbers-work.md)

Plain-language companion to `one-pager-numbers.md`. Structure first, then the loop, then payback, then the plan. Numbers are September 2026, app route, gross unless marked cash.

## 1. What is sold

- A subscription. Two ways in: a 7-day free trial, or a direct purchase.
- It renews weekly, monthly or yearly. Most new subscribers take weekly. Weekly is 80% of the money.
- Apple and Google process every payment and keep about 25%. Gross = what the customer pays. Cash = the 75% that arrives.

These four facts shape every number below.

## 2. The loop

One month of ad money goes out and comes back through a chain:

spend → trials → subscribers → revenue

Each stage is priced by dividing the month's spend by the survivors at that stage.

| Stage | Price of one | August 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Trial | cost per trial (CPT) | $17 |
| Subscriber | cost per subscriber (CPS) | $40 |

Trials become subscribers at the trial-to-paid rate: 48% iOS, 27% Android, 42% blended. CPS = CPT ÷ trial-to-paid.

CPT is the ad's number: an ad's job ends at trial start. Trial-to-paid is the product's number.

## 3. Cost vs value

A trial costs $17 up front. Its value arrives in installments, from whoever converts and stays:

| Time since trial | Worth of one trial |
|---|---|
| 30 days | about $5.50 |
| 12 months | about $22 (cash: $16) |

Return on ad spend (ROAS) = value ÷ cost. Same trial, two readings: 32% at month 1, 129% at month 12.

Month 12 is month 1 plus eleven smaller collections, because people leave. The sum is about 3x, not 12x.

**The rules.** A month's spend must return 100% by month 12 (company rule, gross). At least 32% by month 1 (VC rule). They are the same bar read at two moments.

**The ceiling.** At $22 per trial, month-12 ROAS is exactly 100%. Never buy a trial above $22. Cash break-even is $16.

Every cohort is in loss at month 1 by construction. The question is only whether the installments close it by month 12.

## 4. The level

MRR is the pile of all monthly paychecks, read on a date like a bank balance. $2.55M gross, $1.90M cash. ARR = MRR × 12: a speed, not a year's earnings.

Only two things move it: paychecks joining (the loop) and paychecks leaving (the leak).

**The leak** = pile at start + MRR added − pile at end, measured on a past month. About 20% of the pile drains each month. It is a share, not a fixed amount: a bigger pile drains more.

## 5. The plan

To raise the pile, pour in the leak first, then the growth:

new MRR to add = leak + growth needed

Then walk down to what an ad delivers:

| Step | Divide or multiply by | Month 1 of the $5M plan |
|---|---|---|
| New MRR to add | $510k leak + $690k growth | $1.2M |
| New subscribers | ÷ $18 per new paycheck | 67k |
| Trials | ÷ 42% trial-to-paid | 160k |
| Spend | × $17 CPT | $2.7M |

Each month needs more than the last. Check at the bottom: the CPT you would pay must stay under $22, or the growth target and the payback rule contradict each other.

## 6. A creative system's job

It produces **spend capacity**: budget the buyers can deploy at or under target CPT. A winner is an ad that absorbs real spend at ≤ $17, never above $22.

Per-ad visibility ends at trial start. Whether an ad's trials convert better or worse than average cannot be measured per ad today.

## Three things to re-read before a meeting

1. Value grows with the horizon; always attach a window.
2. Month 12 is a sum of shrinking collections, not month 1 × 12.
3. Leak = start + added − end.
