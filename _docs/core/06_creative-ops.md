# Creative Ops Notes

How performance creative work is generally done. Craft knowledge, not company facts: treat as a solid default, not law.

## The layers of an ad

An ad is built in layers, decided in this order: **insight** (what's true about the audience) → **concept/angle** (the persuasive idea) → **hook** (the first 1-3 seconds or the headline) → **format** (the mold: the structure the ad is poured into) → **treatment** (the visual style) → **script** (the words, written last, into the chosen mold). Most failed ads fused these into one "make an ad" step; keeping them separate makes each one fixable on its own.

## Layers age at different speeds

Insights barely expire (human pain is stable). Concepts last months. Formats last a season. Treatments expire in weeks: a visual style stops working precisely when it becomes recognizable as an ad (example: notes-app-screenshot statics converted for years, then died once the pattern saturated). Consequence: anything that collects formats or styles must be a continuous feed, not a library built once.

## Formats are harvested, not invented

Nobody reliably invents winning formats, including strong internal teams: formats are absorbed from platform-native content and copied from competitors' long-running ads (an ad that has run for months is paying for itself; that is public, free evidence). Big consumer-app teams work concept-first: pick the idea, then pour it into 2-3 proven molds from a maintained library. A common budget shape: ~70% iterating on winners, ~20% new concepts in proven molds, ~10% wild swings.

## The hook is its own object

The hook is not the first line of the script; it is a separate asset. It is multimodal (what's on screen, the overlay text, the first spoken words, the sound), it is judged by its own metric (did they stop scrolling), and it is swappable: a proven ad body with five different hooks is five ads. The hook makes a promise; the script keeps it.

## Copy: transpose, don't originate

LLMs asked to write ad copy from nothing produce generic filler, whatever framework they're given. What works: transposition (take a proven script's skeleton, refill it for a new problem), vocabulary grounded in customers' verbatim words, and overgenerate-then-select with concrete criteria (spoken register, no abstract nouns, would a person say this sentence out loud).

## Polish can hurt

Unpolished, native-looking content often beats produced-looking content, because polish pattern-matches to "advertisement" and gets scrolled. The production bar is authenticity, not quality; this favors cheap-looking artifacts over cinematic ones.

## Broad brand, specific ads

The store page and website can present the broad product for everyone; the ads each speak to one person about one problem. These are different jobs and should not be averaged into one voice.

## Name ads so wins are explainable

Encode the layer choices (concept, format, hook) into every ad's name or ledger row. When something wins, you want to know which layer won; unattributable wins teach nothing.
